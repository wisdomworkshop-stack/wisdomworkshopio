Outcomes

What you'll walk out with

We'll explore a variety of ways to live a good life. We'll co-create a fun, graduate-level philosophy seminar focused on doing philosophy with other curious people, and explore how comparative philosophy can be used as a tool to clarify, design, and advance entrepreneurial and life goals.

Participants will finish the course with a written personal philosophy of life — a way of choosing a personal philosophy for work and beyond.

Part philosophy course, part walking group, part container for spiritual exercises, we'll together re-imagine continuing education as a WandelGarten.

A note from Sean

Why World Philosophy Now?

This course addresses my biggest pet peeve with what passes as the serious study of philosophy in America. If you're considering enrolling in a philosophy program, you'd likely get 90% Western Philosophy. But the Greeks and Europeans are just one of the world's approaches to the love of wisdom. (I guess it is a Greek word, after all — philosophy literally means a particular kind of brotherly love — philia — with a particular kind of wisdom, sophia.)

I have had sincere teachers in Buddhist, Taoist, and Islamic philosophy — each who have approaches that have changed my life — so I'm including these here. We'll close in honoring all our relationships via earth-and-plant wisdom of Indigenous philosophies.

But perhaps further: world philosophy is especially important today, where so much of a narrow kind of worldview is baked into frontier AI models and ensuing AI discourse. In other words, we don't need artificial intelligence to flatten our inquiry. We need modes of expanding our inquiries in meaningful ways. We need stronger barometers for what matters before intelligence. Better ways to survey the buildings that our ladders are leaning on.

For more on this, see Michael Millerman's "The Analytic Monopoly: And Why It Isn't Enough."

WTF is a WandelGarten?

If you took a philosophy course in college, you would read other people's words and write some of your own. For us, doing philosophy means not only reading and writing, but also engaging with our feet, walking. We'll encounter each philosophy as a living footpath, a distinct way of moving and changing here and now — how we relate to ourselves, our lives, our others and our world. We'll pay special attention to the wisdom of nature, process, and plants.

Our WandelGarten — a garden of both walking and change — will encourage each of us to honor the diversity of cultures across the globe as we honor the difference of each member of our group.

— Sean Waters, Lead Facilitator
