Why Heider, after Rogers

Carl Rogers told us what a helping person needs to be. Lao Tzu, twenty-five centuries earlier, said much the same thing about leaders, and said it in fewer words.

John Heider led encounter groups for years before he sat down with the Tao Te Ching and rewrote its eighty-one short chapters for people who lead groups. The result is not a scholarly translation. It is a working facilitator's adaptation, one page per chapter. The leader who talks less, trusts the group's process, and takes the lowest seat is Rogers's facilitator in older clothes.

We center verse 67, where Lao Tzu names three treasures he guards and keeps. Each one gives rise to something a leader needs. Each one, reached for without its root, goes wrong.

Key ideas

Three treasures, and the water they float on

Verse 67 lists three treasures. Each comes in several translations, and the spread is worth knowing, because each word lights up a different side.

Deep love → courage

Compassion, mercy

Love that is particular, not general: delight in this person as they are. Because you care about someone, you can be brave on their behalf.

Simplicity → generosity

Frugality, economy

Fewer words, fewer plans, fewer certainties. What you don't spend on yourself is what you have to give.

Humility → leadership

Equality, modesty

Literally, "not daring to be ahead of the world." Neither above nor below the people you serve, but among them.

Water · wu wei

Effortless action

Water takes the shape of its container, seeks the lowest places, and nourishes everything it touches. Wu wei is not no effort; it is no wasted effort.

Easy to miss: the verse ends with a warning. Courage without love, generosity without simplicity, and leadership without humility all fail, however good they look. The treasure comes first.

The treasures, in practice

Heider's leader learns by doing less and noticing more. Each of these works on your own, starting today.

  1. Simplicity: half the wordsIn one meeting or class you lead this week, say half of what you planned to. Notice who fills the space you leave.
  2. Deep love: glad you're hereGreet one person as if you had been waiting for them. Name one particular thing about how they show up that you're glad of.
  3. Humility: speak lastIn one group you hold, speak last on every question. Write down what you heard before you write what you think.
  4. Water: the low placesFind the quiet person in a room, or the task nobody wants, and go there without mentioning it.
