The craft of character, the art of holding space

"True listening requires a setting aside of oneself."

Welcome. You've joined the waitlist for a facilitation masterclass built on a simple conviction: people forget information, but they remember who you are and how you made them feel. This experience is for anyone who facilitates, teaches, coaches, or holds space — and wants to do it with the craft of character and the three treasures of simplicity, honesty, and humility.

Before We Begin

Read

  • The Courage to Teach by Parker Palmer — Chapter 1, "The Heart of a Teacher." On why who you are matters more than what technique you use.
  • The 13 Commitments of Conscious Facilitation — our framework for the course. Read them below and notice which ones you already practice, and which feel like an edge:
  1. Grateful Humility
  2. Personal Responsibility
  3. Coherent Purpose
  4. Psychological Safety
  5. Mindful Presence
  6. Genuine Curiosity
  7. Deep Love
  8. Emotional Intelligence
  9. Courageous Generosity
  10. Agile Improvisation
  11. Cultural Sensitivity
  12. Considerate Honesty
  13. Negative Capability

Write

Spend ten minutes with this prompt:

"Think of the best teacher, facilitator, or leader you've ever experienced. What did they do? What did they not do? And what did it feel like to be in the room with them?"

Listen

This week, facilitate one conversation — at work, at dinner, with friends — where you say almost nothing. Ask one beautiful question, then hold space for the answer. Notice what happens when you resist the urge to fix, advise, or redirect. Bring your observations.

What You'll Practice

Third-generation facilitation: the symmetry between teacher and learner, where the facilitator is also learning. The SLOW compass — four concentric circles of relationship. The arc of a gathering: Kindle, Ground, Connect, Deepen, Grow. How to read a room. How to hold silence. How to trust the group's collective intelligence. How to design experiences where the room writes the curriculum. You are not the sage on the stage. You are not even the guide on the side. You are a fellow human who holds the container.

"Enter into the conversation like you have something to learn."
