Three treasures

A companion to John Heider's The Tao of Leadership, for leading the way water does: low, receptive, and with no wasted effort.

This week's question: How might you guard and keep the treasures of simplicity, deep love, and equality?

Start here

Why Heider, after Rogers

Rogers told us what a helping person needs to be. Lao Tzu, twenty-five centuries earlier, said much the same thing about leaders, and said it in fewer words.

John Heider led encounter groups for years before he sat down with the Tao Te Ching and rewrote its eighty-one short chapters for people who lead groups. The result is not a scholarly translation. It is a working facilitator's adaptation, one page per chapter, and that is exactly why it belongs here. Rogers quoted Lao-tse himself in A Way of Being; Heider shows how far the kinship goes. The leader who talks less, trusts the group's process, and takes the lowest seat is Rogers's facilitator in older clothes.

We center verse 67, where Lao Tzu names three treasures he guards and keeps. Each one gives rise to something a leader needs. Each one, reached for without its root, goes wrong.

"Great egocentricity does not make a person great."Heider's chapter 67, "Three Leadership Qualities."

Key ideas

Three treasures, and the water they float on

Verse 67 lists three treasures. Each comes in several translations, and the spread is worth knowing, because each word lights up a different side.

Deep love → courage

Compassion, mercy

Love that is particular, not general: delight in this person as they are. Because you care about someone, you can be brave on their behalf. In a circle: you say the hard, kind thing.

Simplicity → generosity

Frugality, economy

Fewer words, fewer plans, fewer certainties. What you don't spend on yourself is what you have to give. In a circle: you listen with a beginner's mind, and say only what's needed.

Humility → leadership

Equality, modesty

Literally, "not daring to be ahead of the world." Neither above nor below the people you serve, but among them. In a circle: you lead from inside it.

Water · wu wei

Effortless action

Water takes the shape of its container, seeks the lowest places, and nourishes everything it touches. Wu wei is not no effort; it is no wasted effort. Only dead fish go with the flow.

Easy to miss: the verse ends with a warning. Courage without love, generosity without simplicity, and leadership without humility all fail, however good they look. The treasure comes first. And the treasures feed each other: many people in our circles drew them as a circle, each leading into the next.

Practice + play away from the screen

The treasures, in practice

Heider's leader learns by doing less and noticing more. Each treasure has a practice here, and every one of them needs another person. Choose the one that matches your growing edge.

Simplicity · generosity

Ten minutes, three sentences

Find a partner. One of you speaks for ten minutes about something alive this week. The listener may say three sentences in all: no more. Spend them carefully, or don't spend them. Then swap.

Afterward, three minutes. Speaker: which of the three sentences mattered, and why? Listener: what did you want to say and didn't, and what happened in the space you left?

Beyond the circle: in one meeting you lead this week, say half of what you planned to. Notice who fills the room.

10:00
Deep love · courage

Glad you're here

Choose one person you will see this week. Greet them as if you had been waiting for them, because you have. Name one particular thing about them you're glad of. Not "you're great": the contour of how they show up.

The braver version: with someone who has told you they welcome it, ask permission, then gently question one all-or-nothing story they tell about themselves. Love first, then the challenge. Afterward, ask how it landed.

Humility · leadership

Speak last

In one group you hold this week, speak last on every question. Or hand the opening to someone else and follow them. Afterward, ask one person what the room felt like. Write down what they say before you write what you think.

Water · the low places

Go where the water goes

Water seeks the places others avoid. This week, find the quiet person in a group, or the task nobody wants, and go there without mentioning it. Then ask that person one real question and listen to the whole answer.

Process letter two

The treasures you keep, and the ones you've let slip

A few places to begin. Follow whichever one has heat in it, and leave the rest.

Which treasure is already yours? Tell the story of a time it carried you, and what it made possible for someone else.

Which one have you let slip? Is getting it back a treasure hunt, or a treasure you have to make again?

Where did you reach for the fruit without the root? Courage without love, generosity without simplicity, leading from ahead instead of from within. What happened?

Where are the low places in your community? Who is there, and what would it look like to go to them as water does?

To bring to the circle

How do the three treasures relate to Rogers's three attitudes: deep love to unconditional positive regard, simplicity to empathic listening, humility to genuineness? Test that mapping against your own experience. Then: which treasure will you guard and keep, and what does that look like today?
